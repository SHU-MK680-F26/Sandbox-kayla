# Miles After Five — Voice Guide

Miles After Five is a social run club for people who want to get their miles in, meet new people, and have something to look forward to after work.

## Audience

We're writing for 21–35-year-olds who want to stay active but care just as much about meeting people, exploring their city, and having something social to do after work. The moment they see us is around 5pm. They're either walking out of the office scrolling their phone, or lying in bed scrolling through clubs to join. Every caption should make that person want to show up tonight, or at least save the post so they come next time.

## Personality

- **Active.** We always point toward moving, getting outside, and doing something with the evening. It's broken by flat, passive filler that asks nothing of the reader, like "Just another day."
- **Motivational.** We get people out the door with energy and confidence, never with pressure or guilt. It's broken by lines that imply the reader is failing, like "If you really want it, you would make time" or "Discipline is the only way to success."
- **Fun.** Running with us is the best part of the day and should sound like it. It's broken by empty, overused slogans that could belong to any account, like "Good Vibes Only."

## We say / We never say

| We say | We never say |
|---|---|
| "Clock out. Lace up." | "Just another day" |
| "Meet us after five" | "If you really want it, you would make time" |
| "Run into your evening" | "Discipline is the only way to success" |
| "From 9 to 5 to miles" | "You're slow" |
| "See you at the start line" | "You're not a real runner" |

Also never: fat-shaming language of any kind, or "Good Vibes Only."

## Two example captions

**On-voice:**
> Clock out. Lace up. Meet us after five.

Why: It's short and confident and asks for action without getting too serious, the same qualities the team admires in Nike's "Just Do It."

**Off-voice:**
> Skip your running plans and spend your evening relaxing on the couch.

Why: Miles After Five is about getting outside, moving, and socializing to make the most of the hours after work or school. This caption tells people to do the opposite.

## Hard rules

These rules aren't about taste, and they can't be broken:

1. **No shaming.** Never shame anyone for their fitness level, pace, body, or how often they show up.
2. **Everyone is welcome.** Content stays inclusive of every running and fitness level, from first-timers to regulars.
3. **No health claims.** No health, weight-loss, or body-transformation claims, stated or implied.
4. **No invented facts.** Never invent run times, meeting spots, routes, distances, or other club details. If it isn't written down in `brand/` or `strategy/`, write [NEEDS TEAM CONFIRMATION].

## Voice checklist

A reviewer answers yes or no for any caption. Every answer should be **yes**.

1. Does it invite the reader to *do* something, like show up, lace up, or run?
2. Would a first-time or slower runner feel welcome reading it?
3. Is the motivation free of guilt, pressure, or "you're not trying hard enough"?
4. Does it sound like us and not a generic slogan any account could post?
5. Is it free of health, weight-loss, and body claims, and of invented club details?
